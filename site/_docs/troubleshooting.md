# Troubleshooting & Frequently Asked Questions

[&larr; Back to Overview]({{ '/' | relative_url }})

---

## 1. Windows says "Windows protected your PC" (SmartScreen)

**Why it happens:** Community open-source installers that are not signed with a paid Microsoft Authenticode certificate trigger Microsoft Defender SmartScreen until the release builds download reputation.

**What to do:**
1. Verify you downloaded `Wherezy-Setup-2.2.2.exe` directly from the official [GitHub Releases](https://github.com/shadowamitendu/wherezy/releases) page (and optionally compare the release SHA-256 checksum).
2. In the SmartScreen popup, click **More info**, then click **Run anyway**.

---

## 2. Browser shows "Your connection is not private" on HTTPS

**Why it happens:** When you turn on Wherezy's built-in **HTTPS**, it generates a local **self-signed X.509 certificate** on your machine because browsers do not automatically trust private LAN IPs without a public Certificate Authority.

**What to do:**
- **Check the fingerprint (optional):** Open the **Server Logs** pane inside the Desktop Controller (or the CLI startup banner) to read `TLS SHA-256 fingerprint: ...` and compare it with your browser's certificate viewer.
- Click **Advanced &rarr; Proceed to ... (unsafe)** once on that computer or phone browser.
- Want zero warnings across every family phone? Use custom PEM certificates (`--ssl-certfile` / `--ssl-keyfile`) generated by **mkcert** or put **Caddy / Tailscale Serve** in front. See [Profiles, Roles & HTTPS]({{ '/security-and-profiles/' | relative_url }}).

---

## 3. Phone camera scanner says HTTPS / secure context required

**Why it happens:** Mobile Safari and Chrome block live camera video streams (`navigator.mediaDevices`) on plain `http://192.168.x.x` addresses.

**Options to fix it:**
1. **Enable HTTPS (Recommended):** In the Desktop Controller's **Server & Startup Settings**, turn on **Enable HTTPS (TLS)** and set **Host** to `0.0.0.0`, then open `https://<your-lan-ip>:8000` on your phone.
2. **Use HTTP fallback immediately:** On `/scan`, tap **Take Photo of Code** inside **Scan from Photo** to decode a QR code or barcode without requiring HTTPS.
3. **Use native phone Camera QR scan:** Point your phone's built-in Camera app at any printed Wherezy QR sticker (`/#/i/<id>` or `/#/l/<id>`).

---

## 4. Port `8000` is already in use

If another local developer tool or service already listens on `8000`:
- **Desktop Controller:** Click **Pick a Free Port & Start** right in the error banner &mdash; Wherezy finds the next open port, updates your config, and starts automatically.
- **CLI / Docker:** Pass `--port 8010` or set `WHEREZY_PORT=8010` (`PORT=8010`).

## 5. Phones on Wi-Fi cannot connect (`127.0.0.1` vs `0.0.0.0`)

1. Check the Desktop Controller **Host** setting: if it is **`127.0.0.1`**, only your PC can reach Wherezy. Switch Host to **`0.0.0.0`**, click **Save & Apply Settings**, and open the **LAN / Phone:** URL shown under **Endpoints** (or click **Copy LAN URL**).
   - If printing QR labels for phones to scan directly, verify **Settings &rsaquo; Server Address for QR Codes** (`/settings`) matches your PC's Wi-Fi URL.
2. **Windows Firewall popup:** When binding `0.0.0.0` for the first time, Windows asks whether to allow access on **Private networks** (your home Wi-Fi) or **Public networks**. Allow **Private networks** for family devices at home.
3. Verify your computer and phone are on the same Wi-Fi subnet and not isolated by guest Wi-Fi client isolation.

## 6. How do I restore a backup (or roll back an upgrade)?

Wherezy gives you two layers of backup protection inside `<Data Folder>/backups/`:
- **Automatic pre-upgrade snapshots:** Whenever a newer version applies SQLite schema migrations, Wherezy first writes `backups/pre-upgrade-v<from>-to-v<to>-<timestamp>.zip` containing `inventory.db` (created safely via SQLite's online backup API), `photos/`, and `files/`.
- **Manual backups:** Created or managed from **Settings &rsaquo; Backups &amp; Storage** (`/settings`).

**To restore from Admin Settings:**
- Open **Settings &rsaquo; Backups &amp; Storage** as an **Admin** and click **Restore Backup (.zip)** to upload any `.zip` archive.

**To restore manually (if locked out or rolling back an upgrade):**
1. **Stop** Wherezy completely (use Tray **Quit Wherezy** or `Wherezy.exe --quit`).
2. Back up your current `<Data Folder>` copy elsewhere just in case.
3. Extract `inventory.db`, `photos/`, and `files/` from the desired `.zip` into your `<Data Folder>`, replacing the current files.
4. Start Wherezy again.

---

## 7. How do I move my Data Folder safely?

1. Stop the Wherezy server.
2. Copy (rather than move, until verified) your existing Data Folder (`inventory.db`, `photos/`, `files/`, `backups/`) to the new drive or folder.
3. In the **Desktop Controller**, click **Browse** next to **Data Dir:**, pick the new folder, and click **Save &amp; Apply Settings** (Wherezy will prompt **Relaunch Required** so the database engine binds cleanly to the new path).
4. Once verified in the browser, you can remove the old copy.

## 8. Where are crash & startup logs stored?

Desktop and headless file logs use a rotating handler writing `wherezy.log`:
- **Windows:** `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Wherezy\logs\wherezy.log`
- **macOS:** `~/Library/Application Support/Wherezy/logs/wherezy.log`
- **Linux / XDG:** `~/.local/state/wherezy/logs/wherezy.log` (or `$XDG_STATE_HOME/wherezy/logs/wherezy.log`)

You can also view live logs inside the **Desktop Controller** log drawer (or `docker compose logs -f wherezy` in Docker).
